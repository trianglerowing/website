A regatta is a rowing competition. Clubs from many places bring their boats on trailers, race in many events through the day, and go home. For parents, a regatta is a long day outdoors with a lot of waiting and a few exciting minutes. This guide helps you know where to be and what to bring. Rowing words are explained in the [glossary](/new-to-rowing/glossary/).

## Two kinds of races

### Head races (fall)

A head race is a long race, usually about 5,000 meters, or three miles. Boats do not start side by side. They leave the start one at a time, with a gap of several seconds between them, and each boat is timed over the same course. The fastest time wins, even if that boat was not first across the line. Many clubs hold head races in the fall.

At a head race, you will see boats pass the same spot in a long line. Watch for the number the crew carries so you can tell which boat is yours.

### Sprint races (spring)

A sprint race is 2,000 meters, about 1.25 miles. Several boats start at the same time, side by side, each in its own lane, and the first across the finish line wins. Many clubs hold sprint races in the spring. A regatta may have heats in the morning, with the fastest boats moving on to a final later in the day.

## How to follow the schedule and results

A regatta posts a heat sheet, a schedule that lists each race, its start time and the crews in it. Most regattas post their heat sheets and results online a few days before and during the event. Check your child's race number and time. Races may run early or late, so keep checking.

## A race-day timeline

Times differ by regatta. This is a typical order of the day.

1. Early morning: the crew arrives at the boathouse or at the meeting place and loads the trailer. TODO(coach): What time and where do families meet for travel?
2. Arrival: the team unloads boats and sets up the parent tent and the team area.
3. Boat check and warm-up: rowers carry boats to the water, check the boat and warm up.
4. Race: the crew launches, rows to the start and races. Parents move to the course to watch.
5. After the race: the crew returns, puts boats away and cools down.
6. Between races: rest, eat, drink water and wait for the next race.
7. End of day: the team loads the trailer and heads home.

## What to bring

For rowers:

* Team uniform and any spirit wear listed by coaches
* Water bottle, snacks and a packed lunch
* Warm layers, rain jacket and a change of clothes
* Sunscreen, hat and sunglasses
* Any medicine and a written plan for it

For parents:

* Folding chair or blanket
* Warm layers, rain gear and sunscreen
* Water, food and snacks to share
* A phone charger or battery pack
* Comfortable shoes for walking to the course

## How to watch

Rowing courses are long, and there may be no seats near the start. Look for where the crew will pass by. Ask a veteran parent which spot has a good view. Listen for coaches, and keep out of the way of boat carts and trailers. Cheering is welcome. Walking into roped-off areas near the water or the dock is not.

## The trailer and volunteer jobs

The club carries its boats on a trailer. Loading and unloading is hard work and uses many hands. Regattas also need parent volunteers for jobs such as helping with the trailer, working the team tent, handing out food or timing and scoring. Families earn volunteer tokens by helping.
