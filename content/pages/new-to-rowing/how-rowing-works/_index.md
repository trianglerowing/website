Rowing has its own equipment and words. This page covers the boats, the stroke and racing. The [glossary](/new-to-rowing/glossary/) explains the words, and USRowing has more at its [Rowing 101 guide](https://usrowing.org/learn-about-rowing/overview/rowing-101).

## The boats

Boats are named for the number of rowers and whether there is a coxswain (the person who steers and calls the race).

![Rowing boats from a single to an eight, with their lengths and weights](/images/how-rowing-works/rowing-shells.jpg)

**Sculling boats** have two oars per rower, one in each hand.

* Single (1x): one rower, no coxswain.
* Double (2x): two rowers, no coxswain.
* Quad (4x): four rowers, no coxswain.

**Sweep boats** have one oar per rower.

* Pair (2-): two rowers, no coxswain.
* Four (4+): four rowers and a coxswain.
* Straight four (4-): four rowers, no coxswain.
* Eight (8+): eight rowers and a coxswain.

Most rowing at TRC is sweep rowing in pairs, fours and eights. For more about coxswains, see the [Parent FAQ](/new-to-rowing/parent-faq/).

## The stroke

Rowing looks like an upper-body sport, but most of the power comes from the legs. A stroke has four parts.

1. **Catch:** the rower sits forward on the sliding seat with bent knees and straight arms, and drops the blade into the water.
2. **Drive:** the legs push first. Then the back opens and the arms pull the blade through the water.
3. **Finish:** the handle is pulled in and down, lifting the blade out of the water. The rower turns the blade flat. This is the release.
4. **Recovery:** the arms, body and seat return to the start so the rower can catch again.

## How racing works

**Sprint races** are mostly in spring and summer. They are usually 2,000 meters (about 1.25 miles) on a course with 6 to 8 lanes, marked every 500 meters. Junior races are sometimes 1,500 meters. All boats start together. A crew gets one false start, and a second means disqualification.

**Head races** are mostly in fall. They are time trials of about 3,500 to 5,000 meters. Boats start 10 to 15 seconds apart, race once and are ranked by time. A boat can be penalized for leaving the course markers.

Sprint regattas usually have heats followed by finals. Big regattas may add time trials, a second-chance race (repechage), semifinals and several finals (A, B, C).

### Reading an event name

An event name has four parts: gender, level, size and boat type. Examples:

* Women's U17 2-: a pair for girls under 17, no coxswain.
* Men's Youth 4x: a quad for boys under 19, no coxswain.
* Women's Youth 4+: a four for girls under 19, with a coxswain.
* Men's U17 4-: a four for boys under 17, no coxswain.

* **Gender:** men's or women's.
* **Level:** Youth, U17, U16 or U15. "U" means under. Youth means under 19 (also written U19) and still in high school.
* **Size:** the number of rowers: 1, 2, 4 or 8.
* **Type:** x is sculling, + is sweep with a coxswain, - is sweep without one, and x+ is a sculling boat with a coxswain.

### Watching a race

Race times change with the course and weather. A tailwind helps, and a headwind or crosswind slows crews. Good crews look smooth: the oars enter and leave the water together and the boat speed stays steady. A "crab" is when a blade gets stuck in the water and brakes the boat. It looks bad but is common for new rowers. A "Power 10" is a coxswain's call for ten hard strokes. TRC oar blades are orange with a dark blue stripe in the middle. Heat sheets and results are posted online, often on [RegattaCentral](https://www.regattacentral.com) and [HereNow](https://www.herenow.com/results/#/races). Schedules change, so check for updates and leave time to park.

For race-day logistics, read the [regatta guide](/new-to-rowing/regatta-guide/).
